Opening new paths for First Nations, Inuit and Métis entrepreneurs

Find the government funding and contracts worth your time — matched to your business.

Ohahase pulls open government tender and funding data into one place, filters it for Indigenous set-asides and preferences, and explains in plain language why a given opportunity does or does not fit your business.

Ohahase is Kanien’kéha (Mohawk) for new path — because that is the work.

Why it exists

The federal government has committed to directing at least 5% of contract value to Indigenous businesses through the Procurement Strategy for Indigenous Business. Meeting that target depends on Indigenous suppliers actually finding the notices they are eligible for — which today means watching several portals that were never designed to be watched together.

A great deal of the most relevant work never reaches those portals at all. First Nations infrastructure, housing and community RFPs are often posted once, on one organisation’s own website, and the businesses best placed to bid on them simply never hear about it.

One place to look

Federal grants, contributions and CanadaBuys tenders, gathered and kept current.

Matched, not just listed

Filtered against what your business actually does, so the list stays short and real.

Explained in plain language

Every match says why it fits — and when it doesn’t, it says that too.

If we’ve written to you about your RFPs

Ohahase only lists opportunities from sources that have either published under an open licence or told us in writing that we may. Where the rights aren’t clear, we ask first — even when the information is sitting there in public.

When an organisation says yes, what we show is the title, the closing date and a link straight back to the original posting. We don’t reproduce tender documents, and every link sends people to you. If you’d rather simply tell us when something goes up, that works too.

Who’s behind it

Ohahase is a service of Gemini Matrix Consulting Inc., an Indigenous-owned company based in Ohsweken, Six Nations of the Grand River. It is built from inside the community it serves rather than sold into it from outside.
